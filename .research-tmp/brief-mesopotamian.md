# MESOPOTAMIAN MYTHOLOGY — CULTURE-SPECIFIC BRIEF
Framing: historical mythology/religion of Sumer, Akkad, Babylon, Assyria (c. 3000 BCE – 539 BCE, with continuations). The oldest attested mythological tradition in the world. Cuneiform tablets are the primary sources.
Primary sources: Enuma Elish (Babylonian creation epic, c. 1100-700 BCE), Gilgamesh Epic (Sumerian originals c. 2100 BCE, Standard Babylonian version c. 1200 BCE), Atrahasis (c. 1800 BCE), Inanna/Ishtar Descent (Sumerian, c. 1900 BCE), The Curse of Agade, various temple hymns, king lists. Translations: British Museum, University of Penn, Sacred Texts Project, various university resources.
Core entities (must include): Anu (sky), Enlil (air/storm), Enki/Ea (water/wisdom), Inanna/Ishtar (love/war/sexuality — note the Sumerian/Babylonian name change), Utu/Shamash (sun/justice), Nanna/Sin (moon), Enki's children, Tiamat (primordial sea — female), Apsu (fresh water — male), Ki/Earth, Marduk (Babylonian chief god, rises to supremacy in Enuma Elish), Ea, Nergal (underworld), Ereshkigal (queen of underworld), Gugalanna (monster), Humbaba (monster), the Anunnaki (the divine council — note this is a collective, not a single entity).
Critical variants to document:
- Inanna vs. Ishtar: same deity in Sumerian vs. Babylonian — this IS a real case of a deity being known by different names across cultures. Document this clearly as a case study.
- The rise of Marduk: in the Enuma Elish, Marduk replaces Enlil as supreme — this is a political/theological shift, not a mythological "fact." Note this.
- Enki's role: in Sumerian tradition he is one of the three great gods (Anu, Enlil, Enki); in later Babylonian tradition his role diminishes.
- The Sumerian vs. Babylonian afterlife: Sumerian underworld (Kur/Irkalla) is gloomy and universal; later Babylonian tradition adds more nuance.
- Apsu vs. Tiamat: the primordial pair — Apsu is fresh water, Tiamat is salt water/chaos. Their conflict is the basis of the Enuma Elish.
Realms: the three-tiered cosmos (Anu's sky above, Enlil's earth/atmosphere, Enki's underworld/appears in the Abzu), the Underworld (Kur/Irkalla), the celestial realm, the Abzu (primordial waters), the temple ziggurat as axis mundi.
Museum art: Mesopotamian art is ancient (3000-539 BCE) — all PUBLIC DOMAIN. British Museum (Wander of Gilgamesh, various reliefs), Louvre (Ishtar Gate — actually in Berlin Pergamon Museum; use British Museum Mesopotamian collection), Met (Open Access — various Mesopotamian objects), Iraq Museum (Baghdad — many objects damaged/lost in 2003; verify availability), Istanbul Archaeology Museums, the Pergamon Museum (Ishtar Gate — verify access). Cylinder seals, stone reliefs, statues (Wander of Gudea), the Stele of Hammurabi (Louvre).
Mandatory myths: Enuma Elish (creation + Marduk's rise), Inanna's/Ishtar's Descent into the Underworld, Gilgamesh (the epic — focus on the mythological elements: Enkidu, the Flood, the quest for immortality), Atrahasis (the flood tradition). Add: the conflict of Apsu and Tiamat (part of Enuma Elish), the curse of Agade (optional).
