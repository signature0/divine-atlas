# SLAVIC MYTHOLOGY — CULTURE-SPECIFIC BRIEF
FRAMING (critical): Slavic mythology is largely RECONSTRUCTED. There are NO major primary mythological texts in any Slavic language (unlike Greek/Norse/Japanese). Evidence comes from:
1. Non-Slavic sources describing Slavic gods (Byzantine, Arabic, German, Russian chronicles) — 9th-13th c.
2. Slavic folk traditions, toponyms, ethnographic records (19th-20th c.)
3. Comparative Indo-European linguistics
This means the "mythology" is largely scholarly reconstruction. State this clearly. Do NOT present reconstructed myths as if they were recorded myths.
Primary sources:
- Primary (non-Slavic): Ibn Rusta (10th c., on Slavic gods including Veles, Stribog), Constantine VII Porphyrogennitus (10th c., De Ceremoniis — lists Slavic gods), Adam of Bremen (11th c., on the Rostov/Slavic temple), various Byzantine and Russian chronicles
- Slavic: folk collections (19th-20th c.), toponymic studies, comparative studies
- Comparative: IE comparative mythology (Beekes, Pokorny, etc.)
Core entities (must include — all with reconstruction notes): Perun (thunder/war), Veles (underworld/water/ cattle/poison), Stribog (wind), Svarog/Svarozhich (fire/smithing/sky), Dazhbog/Dazhbog (sun), Moko/Mokosh (fertility/water/moon — gender disputed), Chors (horse god), Jarilo (spring/youth), Mokosh, Lada/Lada (beauty/love), the Zory/Zorya (dawn/twilight sisters), Baba Yaga (spirit/monster — note: folk tale, not strictly mythological), Koshchey Bessmertny (folk), the Veles-Perun dichotomy (the core scholarly debate).
Critical scholarly debates to document:
- Perun vs. Veles: which was the chief god? Most scholars: Perun was the chief state god (Byzantine sources), Veles was older and more widespread in folk tradition. This is the central question of Slavic mythology.
- Mokosh's gender: originally female (Mokosh/Mokoshy) — some reconstructions make her male (Mokosh → Mokoš as a male sky god). Document the debate.
- The Veles-Perun opposition: often compared to the Dionysus-Zeus or Osiris-Set patterns — note that this is a scholarly INTERPRETATION, not attested.
- Baba Yaga: folk tale character, not a deity — include but mark as folklore, not mythology.
- The absence of a written mythological tradition: this is the most important framing note.
Realms: the three-world cosmology (upper/middle/lower world) is a RECONSTRUCTION based on IE patterns — note this. The world tree (World Tree / Axis Mundi) is reconstructed from folk evidence.
Museum art: Slavic mythological art is almost entirely 19th-20th c. (Romantic/Nationalist period). V. Vasnetsov (Russian, 19th c. — many works in Tretyakov Gallery, verify PD status: Vasnetsov d. 1927 → PD in many jurisdictions), M. Nesterov, I. Bilibin (illuminations, 1920s — may still be in copyright in some jurisdictions, verify), V. M. Vasnetsov's "Baba Yaga" and "Knight at the Crossroads" are iconic. Also: Slavic ornamental embroidery, wood carvings (ethnographic museums), pre-Christian Slavic archaeological finds (idol temples — few survive).
Mandatory myths: Perun and Veles (the core opposition — present as reconstructed), the seasonal cycle (Zorya, Jarilo, the solar year), available reconstructed traditions (the world tree, the three-world structure). Be HONEST: there are very few "myths" in the narrative sense — most are reconstructed patterns. State this.
