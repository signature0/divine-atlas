# JAPANESE MYTHOLOGY / SHINTO — CULTURE-SPECIFIC BRIEF
FRAMING (critical): Shinto is a LIVING religious tradition, not historical fiction. Treat it with the same respect as any living religion. This is "mythological tradition of a living religion" — do NOT frame as "fictional mythology." Note explicitly in sources.md framing section.
Primary sources: Kojiki (712 CE), Nihon Shoki (720 CE) — these are the two major mythographic compilations; also Norito liturgy, Engishiki (927), various local shrine records. Translations: Sacred Texts Project (Kojiki translation), University of Hawaii press editions.
Core entities (must include): Izanagi, Izanami, Amaterasu (Amaterasu-ōmikami), Tsukuyomi, Susanoo, Konohanasakuya-hime, Ninigi, Ōkuninushi, Kotohime, the eight primordial kami (Kurozu, etc. — pick the main ones), Ame-no-Uzume, Takemikazuchi, Kagutsuchi, Yamata-no-Orochi (monster), the sun goddess's siblings. Also: Shining Sun Goddess, Moon God, Storm God.
Critical Kojiki vs Nihon Shoki variants to document:
- Order of birth: Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, Susanoo (standard) — but some passages/variants reorder.
- Ōkuninushi: the "giving up" of the earth — multiple versions of how he was displaced (Kotohime's scheme, the bet, the divine marriage).
- Amaterasu's descent: the Amaterasu myth is well-attested; the details of the heavenly cave differ between Kojiki and Nihon Shoki.
- Susanoo's exile: reasons and details vary.
- Izanagi/Izanami: the creation of islands — the Kojiki lists specific islands in order; Nihon Shoki differs slightly.
Realms: Takamagahara (High Plain of Heaven), Ashihara-no-Nakatsukuni (Middle Land of the High Plains / the human world), Yomi (Land of the Dead), the underground/sea realms.
Museum art: Tokyo National Museum (Shinto ritual objects, bronze mirrors, kofun period artifacts), Kyoto National Museum, Freer/Sackler (SMIT), ukiyo-e prints (Met Open Access — Hokusai, Hiroshige; note these are Edo period, 17th-19th c., so PD), Meiji-era Shinto paintings (many PD), shrine architecture (photographs — verify license). IMPORTANT: most ancient Japanese mythological art is ritual/functional (bronzes, mirrors, tomb figurines), not "paintings of gods" — be honest about this.
Mandatory myths: Izanagi and Izanami (creation + Izanami's death), Birth of the islands, Amaterasu and the heavenly cave, Susanoo and Yamata-no-Orochi, Ōkuninushi and the descent of Ninigi. Add: Izanagi's purge (misogi), the birth of Kagutsuchi.
