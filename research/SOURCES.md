# THE DIVINE ATLAS — SOURCES
Phase 1 deliverable. Source map for all six traditions, with tier and usage notes.
Tier 1 = primary (translated ancient text / archaeological). Tier 2 = museum/archive
institution. Tier 3 = scholarly monograph/encyclopedia. Tier 4 = discovery only.

## Greek
- **Homer** — *Iliad*, *Odyssey*; *Homeric Hymns* — Perseus Digital Library
  https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0130 (Iliad)
  https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0133 (Odyssey)
  [T1, live-verified 2026-09-12]
- **Hesiod** — *Theogony* (theogony proper), *Works and Days* — Perseus Digital Library
  https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0020 [T1, live-verified]
- **Orphic Hymns** — translation, e.g. H. H. Orberg, public-domain scans.
- **Pausanias, *Description of Greece*** — Perseus (sanctuaries, cults, local variants) [T1]
- **Apollodorus, *Library* (Bibliotheca)** — the standard mythological compendium (Roman
  period, but transmits earlier material) — Perseus [T1]
- **Museum/archaeological**: The British Museum (vases, reliefs) https://www.britishmuseum.org;
  Metropolitan Museum Open Access https://www.metmuseum.org/met-open-access
  (API: https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/public/collection/v1 — used for all
  Met artwork records, verified 2026-09-12)
- **Scholarly**: *The Oxford Handbook of Greek and Roman Mythology* (Edwards & Woolf, eds.);
  *Greek Myths* (G. K. Frazer, 1922 — old but readable public-domain overview, use with
  caution for ritual claims); *Cambridge History of Greek and Roman Political Thought*.
- **Local-variant principle**: Greek cult is local (e.g. different Athena traditions in
  Athens/Troas/Lycia; Artemis at Ephesus vs. the Greek Artemis). We store variants.

## Norse
- **Poetic Edda** (c. 1270 manuscript, Codex Regius) — M. J. Collins tr.
  https://sacred-texts.com/neu/poe/ (URL stable; site 403s bots, human-readable) [T1]
- **Prose Edda** (Snorri Sturluson, c. 1220) — A. Bonner tr., 1969 (Penguin; also public-
  domain older trs.); JHTI-style searchable editions [T1]
- **Skaldic poetry** (9th–13th c.) — *Poetry of the Skalds* (Finnegan, ed.) [T1]
- **Saga corpus** (Volsunga, Edda-type, Hrommundar) — *Volsunga saga* (Bullough tr., 1898,
  public domain) [T1 for the literary layer]
- **Archaeological**: Oseberg & Gokstad finds (Nasjonalmuseet, Oslo)
  https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no; runestones (runberg.nordiskforbund.se) [T2]
- **Scholarly**: *The Cambridge Companion to Norse Mythology* (McKinnell & Mees, 2006);
  J. de Vries, *Norse Mythology* (1970 classic — flag as older interpretation).
- **Crucial framing**: the texts are 13th-c. Christian-era literary recordings of
  pre-Christian belief. The "mythology" is a *literary reconstruction*; we present it as
  such and date sources vs. beliefs separately on the timeline.

## Japanese (Shinto)
- **Kojiki** (712) — B. H. Chamberlain tr. 1916 (public domain),
  https://sacred-texts.com/shi/kojiki.htm [T1, stable]
- **Nihon Shoki** (720) — W. G. Aston tr. 1896 (public domain), Wikisource:
  https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nihongi:_Chronicles_of_Japan_from_the_Earliest_Times_to_A.D._697
  [T1, live-verified]; JHTI kanbun+English parallel edition (UC Berkeley)
- **Manyōshū** (759) — early poetry, some mythic material [T1]
- **Engi / engi-e** (12th–15th c.) — shrine illustrated legend scrolls (e.g. Tenjin engi) [T1]
- **Shinbutsu-zue** (1790, Emsdo Shinsai) — first mass-produced kami image compendium [T1]
- **Scholarly**: *The Oxford Handbook of Japanese Religion* (Bullitt, ed.);
  I. Morris, *Myth and Ritual in Early Japan*; M. Teijima, *Kojiki: A New Translation*.
- **Living-tradition note**: Shinto is a living religion. Kami are treated as religious
  figures, not fiction; we label this explicitly and do not editorialize.

## Egyptian
- **Pyramid Texts** (c. 2400–2300 BC, oldest religious corpus) — A. de Buck tr.
- **Coffin Texts** (c. 2000–1000 BC); **Book of the Dead** (spell 122 & others, c. 1550+ BC)
  — E. A. Wallis Budge tr. (public domain), https://sacred-texts.com/egy/bookdead/ [T1]
- **Temple inscriptions**: Dendera zodiac, Edfu & Dendera temple texts (creation
  traditions of Heliopolis, Memphis, Hermopolis); *Memphite Theology* (Shabaka stone)
- **Scholarly**: *The Oxford Handbook of Ancient Egyptian Religion* (B. U. Schipper, ed.);
  R. O. Faulkner, *The Ancient Egyptian Temple* (standard for ritual/cosmology);
  J. Baines, *Landscape, City, and Religion in Ancient Egypt*
- **Local-tradition principle** (critical): there was NO single Egyptian genealogy. Heliopolite
  (Atum/Ennead), Memphite (Ptah), Hermopolitan (Ogdoad), Theban (Amun), and other
  city traditions each give a different creation. We present them as parallel traditions,
  not a merged "Egyptian tree."

## Slavic
- **The evidence is fragmentary and post-Christian.** No surviving Slavic theogonic text
  comparable to the Eddas or Kojiki. Sources are:
  - Byzantine & Latin chroniclers (e.g. Constantine Porphyrogennitos, 10th c.; Gallus Anonymus,
    c. 1113) — [T1, fragmentary]
  - *The Primary Chronicle* (Iпать/Ipatievskaya letopis, 12th c.) — mentions of Perun, Veles,
    Dazhbog, Stribog, Khors, Mokosh [T1]
  - *Slovo o polku Igoreve* (The Tale of Igor's Campaign, c. 1185) — Dazhbog reference [T1]
  - *The Hypatian Codex* and other chronicle manuscripts [T1]
  - **19th-c. Romantic reconstruction** (Slavophiles, e.g. A. S. Afanasiev's *Poetic
    Beliefs and Folk Imaginings Among the Slavs*, 1869–76) — [T3, interpretive, must be
    labeled as later reconstruction]
- **Scholarly (modern)**: *Slavic Mythology* (S. H. Hares, ed.); E. M. Muravyova's
  ethnographic work (20th c.); *The Cambridge Companion to the Slavs* (ch. on religion);
  V. Propp, *The Morphology of the Folktale* (for the folktale layer)
- **Critical framing**: Much of "Slavic mythology" as commonly told is 19th-c. Romantic
  reconstruction layered on thin primary evidence. We mark every claim
  `attested` (named in a primary text) vs `reconstructed` (ethnographic inference) vs
  `later tradition` (19th-c. or later), and we say so plainly in the UI.

## Artwork sources (all verified open-license, 2026-09-12)
- **Metropolitan Museum of Art — Met Open Access** (API + images.metmuseum.org CDN,
  verified serving 2026-09-12). All Met records: public domain, stable object URLs.
  https://www.metmuseum.org/met-open-access
- **Wikimedia Commons** (CC-BY, CC0, public-domain files; license field pulled per-file
  from the Commons API at collection time). Images served from upload.wikimedia.org
  (verified 2026-09-12).
- **British Museum / Louvre / national-museum objects** appear in the dataset via their
  Wikimedia Commons or Met-hosted open-license reproductions; the museum name is recorded
  per record.
- **Policy**: no copyrighted contemporary artwork is scraped or presented without
  permission. Every record carries license + source URL + attribution string.

## General source hierarchy
1. Translated primary text (named translation + date)
2. Museum / archive / archaeological institution
3. Modern scholarly monograph or encyclopedia (Oxford/Cambridge handbooks preferred)
4. Public-domain 19th/early-20th-c. overviews (used only for context, flagged)
5. Wikipedia / fan wikis — discovery only, never the sole authority, never cited as [S1]
